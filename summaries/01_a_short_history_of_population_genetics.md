# Chapter 1. A Short History of Population Genetics

This chapter is a history of population genetics told through the statistical questions each period left open. It follows Darwin's difficulty with blending inheritance, Mendel's ratios and their chi square test, the quarrel between the biometricians and the Mendelians, and the synthesis of Fisher, Haldane and Wright. It then covers Kimura's diffusions and the neutral theory, the sampling theory of Ewens and Kingman, the coalescent, and the genomic era of biobanks and polygenic traits. Six worked examples use numbers small enough to check by hand: Mendel's 3 to 1 ratio, the Hardy Weinberg law, Fisher's decomposition of the genetic variance, Haldane's 2s, the Ewens sampling formula and the expected times of the coalescent. The chapter proves no new theorem. Its purpose is to set the questions that the later chapters take up: what the Fundamental Theorem of Natural Selection says and what it costs, how many parameters a genomic model really has, how neutral alleles are sampled, and how to infer without a likelihood.

## From the literature

The chapter presents work of Darwin, Mendel, Fisher, Haldane, Wright, Kimura, Ewens and Kingman, with the dates and names given in the text. The Hardy Weinberg law is due to Hardy (1908) and Weinberg (1908). Ewens's sampling formula is Ewens (1972) and the coalescent is Kingman (1982).
