# Chapter 12. Open Problems and the Future

This chapter collects the problems that the other chapters leave open, arranged by the three themes of the book: selection and the additive model, the sampling theory of neutral alleles, and inference without a likelihood. As many as possible are stated as precise mathematical or statistical questions, including one conjecture, that no sampling weight depending on atom index and position that charges every interval carries a two parameter Poisson Dirichlet law to a different one, which would make the rigidity theorem of the earlier chapter complete. The chapter then adds the larger questions that the genomic era forces on statistical genetics: samples comparable in size to the population, genealogies inferred at biobank scale, ancient DNA as a time series, the portability of polygenic prediction, and learned inference whose calibration has to be checked and not assumed. Short calculations show the scale of the difficulties: the slow approach of the singleton fraction to Pitman's discount parameter, the gap between marginal and conditional calibration, and the failure of the coalescent when the sample approaches the size of the population.

## From the literature

The chapter draws on the results of the earlier chapters and on the literature they cite, in particular Pitman's discount parameter and the Ewens Pitman family.
